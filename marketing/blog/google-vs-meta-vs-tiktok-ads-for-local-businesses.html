If you’re a local business thinking about paid ads, the first question is usually “which platform?” The honest answer depends less on the platform and more on one thing: are your customers already looking for you, or do you need to put yourself in front of them?

The one difference that matters most

Google Search ads reach people who are already searching. Someone types “water heater repair near me” and your ad appears. They have a problem now and they’re ready to call someone.

Meta (Facebook and Instagram) and TikTok ads reach people who aren’t searching. They’re scrolling. Your ad has to catch their attention and give them a reason to care, often before they knew they wanted what you sell.

Neither is better. They do different jobs.

Google: when people search for what you do

Google is usually the first place to spend if people already search for your kind of business, especially when they need someone soon.

  • Good fit: plumbers, HVAC, electricians, roofers, garage doors, locksmiths, pest control, towing, lawyers, dentists, and most home services.
  • Why: high intent. The person searching is already a likely customer.
  • The catch: popular searches can get expensive per click, because your competitors are bidding on the same words. Tight targeting by town and careful choice of search terms matters a lot.
  • What makes it work: a landing page that matches the ad, a call button, and tracking of calls and forms, so you can see which searches actually bring work.

Meta: when your business is easier to show than to search for

Facebook and Instagram are good at putting something appealing in front of people nearby, then reminding them later.

  • Good fit: restaurants, cafés and bakeries, salons and barbers, fitness studios, events, home improvement with great before-and-after photos, local shops and offers.
  • Why: you can target a radius around your business and show photos or short videos that sell the experience.
  • The catch: people weren’t looking, so the ad has to earn attention. Weak photos and generic text get scrolled past.
  • What makes it work: real photos, a clear offer or reason to act, and showing ads again to people who already visited your site.

TikTok: when you can make short videos people want to watch

TikTok ads work like TikTok itself: short vertical videos that feel native to the feed rather than polished commercials.

  • Good fit: food and drink, beauty, fitness, retail and products, entertainment, anything visual and fun to watch, and businesses whose customers skew younger.
  • Why: it can reach a lot of people for the money when the video is good.
  • The catch: it lives or dies on the video. You need a steady supply of new, real-looking clips, because the same ad wears out quickly.
  • What makes it work: hooks in the first second or two, real people, and filming on a phone rather than overproducing.

Start with one platform, done properly

Spreading a small budget across three platforms usually means none of them gets enough to learn what works. Pick the one that fits best, run it properly for a couple of months, and add a second once the first is working and you can measure it.

Set these up before you spend a dollar

  • Ad accounts in your own name, so you always own the account, the history and the data, even if you change who manages it.
  • Conversion tracking: calls, form messages and bookings counted on your site, tied back to the ad that brought them.
  • A page worth landing on: fast on phones, matching what the ad promised, with a clear way to call or ask for a quote.
  • A clear goal: calls, booked appointments or orders, not just clicks or views.
  • A budget you can sustain for a couple of months while the campaign learns.

How to tell if it’s working

Clicks and impressions don’t pay the bills. What you want to know is: how many calls, forms or bookings did the ads bring, and what did each one cost? Then compare that with what a new customer is worth to you. If a new customer is worth far more than the cost of getting one, it’s working, even if the click prices look high.

Be wary of anyone who guarantees results, rankings or a specific return. Nobody controls the platforms, and honest ad managers talk in tests and measurements, not promises.
